Steve has corrected me 3x in one session for the same tendency: over-qualifying / reaching for definitive proof / hedging findings to death ("thin dataset", "selectivity", and explicitly "you're being that absolutist guy again").

**Why:** Trading is probabilities, never proof. Steve knows there's no definitive proof to be had; the edge is in *finding which factors load onto the outcome (e.g., the pin) on enough days to tilt odds*. Absolutist hedging ("this isn't proof", "n=1", "can't conclude") is noise to him — he already knows the epistemics. It reads as me not committing.

**How to apply:** Lead with the probabilistic read and the actionable edge. State the signal and how to stack factors. One crisp caveat is fine when genuinely load-bearing (e.g., "we captured too late today to test 1pm→settle") — but don't bury a real finding under proof-seeking qualifiers. Be decisive: "here's the likely edge and how to play it," not "here's why we can't be sure." He wants a committed opinionated analyst, not a hedge fund risk-disclaimer. Relates to [[project_pin_projection_research]] (the goal is calibrated probabilities/selectivity, not certainty).
