Steve's self-assessment (2026-07-18, stated before first drill session): above-average
perception of **momentum** and **angles**. His evidence: always the first in the room
to perceive whether a player will get to the ball (reflexive cheer/groan before
anyone else); can eyeball the midpoint of a board to cut with high accuracy.
Explicitly "just above average," not freak-level. He left the trading translation to
Strader's judgment.

**How to apply in drill design:** exploit anticipation — capture his call
*mid-flight* (before the reveal), because trajectory-anticipation is where the
strength lives: will price reach the level, will the rally-back reach the fly
center, is bar pace accelerating. Midpoint-eyeballing maps to range/value-area
midpoints and fly centering. The paired risk: perceptual confidence can outrun the
stage-four confirm — this is the same failure surface as [[direction-inversion-watch]].
Drills should measure the gap between his early call and the confirmed outcome, not
suppress the early call.
