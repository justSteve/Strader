This is **Mancini's** setup, period (Steve's ruling 2026-07-18; letter-corpus
research COO `docs/research/2026-07-16-mancini-trigger-methodology.md`). His
triggers are pure price-behavior plus elapsed time — no order-flow instrument.
The house adaptation section below layers Steve's order-flow read on top
without altering the underlying doctrine.

## Thesis

An obvious support low is lost with force; the break stops paying; buyers
reclaim the level. Everyone who sold the break — stopped-out longs first,
breakout shorts second — is the fuel: their exits power the recovery. Entry is
**event-driven, not level-driven**: the level only becomes tradeable when the
flush-trap-recover event completes at it.

## The four criteria (Mancini's own, systematic order)

1. **A significant low exists.** Three legal definitions: the prior day's low;
   a multi-hour low / a low that goes 20+ points; or a cluster/shelf of lows.
2. **The low flushes.** Classify the flush: **shallow** (2–11 pts) or **deep**
   (20+ pts) — depth sets how much confirmation is required.
3. **Acceptance.** Price demonstrates it wants to be at/above the low: a
   sell-off at the low that *fizzles*. Two forms — back-test, sell, return
   (common on deep flushes); or rip through, back-test the low, recover
   (common on shallow flushes). **"If you rush, you lose"** — even a
   zero-acceptance rip gets a 2–3 minute minimum wait, never an instant entry.
4. **Recovery + stop.** Enter a little above the low on the reclaim; the stop
   ALWAYS goes below the lowest low of the structure. Until that low is taken
   out, sit in the trade — the low, not a dollar figure, defines wrong.

In the drill curriculum's stage vocabulary: flush = stage 1, acceptance =
stages 2–3 (stall + flip), recovery = stage 4 (confirm). Same event, two
dialects.

## The knife rule (hardest rule, cross-cutting)

An **organized, controlled dip** into a planned level may be bid directly. A
**knifing** flush — fast, vertical, headline-driven — is never caught: wait for
the full flush-and-recover event. "No knife catching allowed, ever. ES rallies
70–80% of the time, but when it sells, it sells."

## Variants

- **Level Reclaim** — same logic against a horizontal S/R shelf instead of a
  single low; slower to form (hours to days); same acceptance-then-recovery
  entry. The gentle sibling: the level was lost quietly rather than trapped.
- **Back-Test Long** — broken resistance re-tested from above; never bought on
  first touch — wait for a local FBD or a demonstrated bounce at the shelf.
- **Breakdown Short** — the rarest and weakest inverse; requires a failed
  bounce at the broken shelf first. Mancini states he effectively never takes
  it in a bull regime; the house treats it as study material, not a play.

## Targets and cadence

- **Level-to-level, always.** Target the next mapped level; never expect more
  from any single setup. Runner management beyond T1 only on exceptional room.
- 1–2 trades/day. Entries before 10:00 CT or after 14:00 CT (Mancini: "before
  11am or after 3pm" ET; 11:00–14:00 ET is chop — matches the house
  window-midday avoid). First trade wins → manage the runner and stop; two
  losses → done for the day.

## House adaptation (Steve, via the singleton expression — SGL)

- Expression vehicle: 0DTE SPX single per the **SGL** record (delta-first
  strike table, sizing, escalation, time-scratch).
- **Order flow modifies size, never replaces the price event** (ratification
  pending st-1g3): the completed price event at the level is the gate;
  confirming tape (absorption at the low, delta flip) = full size; neutral
  tape = base size; *active contradiction* — heavy paid aggression still
  through the level — stands the trade down.
- Every stand-down logged and scored **save or miss** against THESE criteria,
  so passed setups grade against our own doctrine (the 2026-07-17 7460C
  lesson: a borrowed gate produced an undecidable stand-down on a completed
  textbook shallow FBD).

## Invalidation

- The lowest low of the structure trades — out, no negotiation.
- Acceptance never forms: the sell at the low keeps following through
  (continuation — the anti-trap; never fade force that is being paid).

## Entry checklist

- [ ] Significant low named under one of the three definitions
- [ ] Flush classified: shallow (2–11) or deep (20+); say its direction out loud
- [ ] Acceptance observed in one of its two forms (or 2–3 min floor on a rip)
- [ ] Knife rule checked: organized dip vs headline knife
- [ ] Recovery printing; next level named as target
- [ ] Tape read: confirming / neutral / contradicting → size per house adaptation

## Management checklist

- [ ] Stop below the lowest low of the structure — moved never
- [ ] T1 at the next level; trim, manage runner per SGL
- [ ] Exit on a re-loss of the recovered low with paid aggression
- [ ] Log the setup (taken or passed) with save/miss grade
