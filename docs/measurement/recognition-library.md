# Post-Entry Recognition Library — V-Day Late-Day Drops

**Source:** 13 confirmed V-days, May 2025 – May 2026
**Generated:** 2026-05-25 from tape_reconstruction, scare_dip_catalog, premium_trajectory

---

## The Fundamental Finding

**Zero reversals out of 149 scare dips across 13 V-days.**

Every intra-recovery pullback > 2pts eventually resolved upward. 84% continued
within 10 minutes. 2% entered extended consolidation (10+ min) before continuing.
14% happened too close to the bell to classify. None reversed.

On a confirmed V-day, scare dips are noise. The stop-loss handles the "what if
this isn't a V-day" question. Once you're in and the bounce is confirmed, every
pullback is a buying opportunity, not a sell signal.

## Scare Dip Field Guide

### Minor Dips (<5pts) — 80 events, 90% continuation

**What you see:** Price drops 2-4 points from a local high over 1-2 minutes.
Premium dips 5-15%.

**What it means:** Noise. Normal volatility during a recovery. Volume during these
dips is 1.16x the pre-dip rate — slightly elevated but not alarming.

**Action:** Hold. These resolve in 1-2 minutes. Do not adjust position.

### Moderate Dips (5-10pts) — 53 events, 83% continuation

**What you see:** Price drops 5-10 points from a local high over 2-5 minutes.
Premium dips 15-35%. This is the zone where the urge to sell is strongest.

**What it means:** Still noise. 83% continue higher within 10 minutes. Volume
during these dips is 1.14x pre-dip — virtually identical to minor dips. The lack
of volume spike distinguishes this from real selling.

**Action:** Hold. The 14:20 dip on May 21 that triggered Steve's sell was in this
tier. Historically, selling here leaves money on the table 83% of the time.

**Key tell:** If volume DROPS during the dip (ratio < 0.90x), it's exhaustion, not
new selling. The 3 consolidation events averaged 0.80x volume during the dip.

### Major Dips (≥10pts) — 16 events, 56% continuation, 6% consolidation, 38% near close

**What you see:** Price drops 10+ points over 3-12 minutes. Premium drops 30-60%.
This is genuinely scary.

**What it means:** Still mostly resolves upward (56% continue, 6% consolidate then
continue, 0% reverse). The 38% "near close" events are dips in the final 5 minutes
where there's no time to judge — but these are irrelevant for position management
because you should already be managing toward the close.

**Action:** Hold unless you're in OTM options and theta is destroying you. If you're
in ITM options, these major dips are where the discipline pays off. The Oct 29 dip
(24.8pts, 43 minutes) was the deepest — and it still resolved upward.

**Anchor examples:**
- Oct 29: 24.8pt dip at T+26min, lasted 43 minutes → still ended with recovery
- Sep 17: 20.8pt dip at T+14min, lasted 16 minutes → continued higher
- May 21: 10.0pt dip at T+55min (14:50), lasted 6 minutes → recovered by close

## Premium Management: The Real Problem

The scare dip catalog shows that PRICE always recovers. But PREMIUM doesn't always
recover, because theta on 0DTE options is brutal.

### The Theta Problem

| Strike Distance from Trough | Avg Close Multiple | Avg Max Drawdown |
|-----------------------------|-------------------:|------------------:|
| -10pts (slightly ITM) | 1.5x | 58% |
| ATM (0pts) | 1.5x* | 70% |
| +5pts (slightly OTM) | 0.7x | 75% |
| +10pts (OTM) | 0.8x | 80% |
| +15pts (deep OTM) | 0.4x | 85% |

*ATM median is only 0.4x — the mean is inflated by Feb 18's 7.0x outlier.

**Key insight:** Slightly ITM calls (-5 to -10pts from trough) give the most
consistent returns (median 1.3x at close). ATM and OTM calls require perfect
timing — they peak early and collapse.

### The Peak-vs-Close Problem

Across the 12 days with usable premium data (excluding Aug 11's 4-min window):

| Metric | At Peak | At Close |
|--------|--------:|---------:|
| Mean multiple | 3.0x | 1.8x |
| Median multiple | 1.8x | 1.4x |

Selling at the peak captures 67% more profit than holding to close. But you can't
know the peak in real-time. What you CAN know:

**Peak timing patterns:**
- On fast-bounce days: premium peaks 10-15 minutes post-entry, then decays
- On standard-bounce days: premium peaks 20-40 minutes post-entry
- On slow-grind days: premium peaks 30-50 minutes post-entry (or near close)

**The 2x rule:** When your position reaches 2x entry premium, you've captured the
median peak multiple. Consider selling half. Historically, only 4/12 days exceeded
2x at close, but 9/12 exceeded 2x at peak. Selling half at 2x locks in the median
outcome while leaving upside exposure.

## Consolidation Patterns

3 distinct consolidation events were detected (10+ minute range-bound periods after
10+ point recovery):

| Date | Duration | Depth | % of Recovery | Outcome |
|------|----------|------:|-------------:|---------:|
| 2025-10-29 | 43min | 24.8pts | 66% | Recovery continued |
| 2025-11-17 | 12-16min | 10.2-10.8pts | 54-57% | Recovery continued |
| 2026-02-18 | 12min | 6.2pts | 96% | Recovery continued |

**Volume signature during consolidation:** 0.80x the pre-consolidation rate.
Volume DECLINES during genuine consolidation. This distinguishes it from a reversal
(which would show increasing volume on the downside tests).

**All 3 resolved upward.** n=3 is too small for statistical confidence, but the
pattern is consistent: declining volume + rangebound price = waiting, not distributing.

**Premium during consolidation:** Theta bleeds premium at approximately 1-3% per
minute on ATM 0DTE options. A 15-minute consolidation costs 15-45% of option value.
This is why ITM options are safer holds — their premium has more intrinsic component
and less theta exposure.

## Volume Climax Context

The volume climax (from the original Greek discovery study) describes what happens
AT ENTRY, not during the hold. But it provides useful context for the recognition
library:

| Climax Ratio | Frequency | Meaning |
|--------------|-----------|---------|
| >5x expected | 2/13 days | Massive selling climax. Expect fast, volatile bounce. |
| 3-5x expected | 5/13 days | Strong climax. Standard to fast bounce. |
| 1.5-3x expected | 4/13 days | Moderate climax. Any bounce speed. |
| <1.5x expected | 2/13 days | Weak/no climax. Expect slow grind. |

**Climax timing relative to trough:**
- Before trough (T-5 to T-2): 5 days — climax was the selling event, trough follows
- At trough (T+0): 3 days — climax and trough are simultaneous
- After trough (T+3 to T+7): 4 days — climax is the initial bounce volume, not selling

When the climax occurs BEFORE the trough, the bounce tends to be faster (mean V5: 2.7
vs 1.4 when climax is after). This makes sense: early climax = selling exhausted before
the turn; late climax = bounce is still building momentum.

## Decision Framework

### At Entry (trough + confirmation)

1. **Check volume climax ratio.** >3x = expect fast bounce. <1.5x = expect slow grind.
2. **Select strike.** For hold-to-close: ITM (-5 to -10pts from trough). For maximum
   upside with higher risk: ATM.
3. **Set stop-loss.** Below the trough. If this isn't a V-day, the stop handles it.

### During the First 10 Minutes

1. **Expect volatility.** Average 3-4 scare dips in this window. All resolve.
2. **Don't sell.** 90% of minor dips and 83% of moderate dips continue higher.
3. **Watch bounce velocity.** If V5 > 2 pts/min, you're in a fast bounce — premium
   will peak in 10-15 minutes. If V5 < 1 pt/min, prepare for a grind.

### At 2x Premium (The Decision Point)

1. **Consider selling half.** 9/12 days hit 2x at peak; only 4/12 held 2x to close.
2. **If fast bounce:** premium likely near peak. Selling half locks in the median.
3. **If slow grind:** premium may still be building. 2x on a slow grind often
   becomes 3-7x. Hold if ITM, sell if OTM (theta will kill OTM options).

### During Consolidation

1. **Check volume.** Declining = consolidation (hold). Increasing on downside = distribution (consider exit).
2. **Check strike.** ITM options survive consolidation. OTM options bleed 1-3%/min.
3. **Duration:** Historical consolidations lasted 12-43 minutes. All resolved upward.

### Final 10 Minutes (14:50+ CT)

1. **Theta accelerates dramatically.** OTM options lose 5-10%/minute.
2. **Exit OTM positions.** Unless price is very close to your strike.
3. **ITM positions can ride to close.** Intrinsic value protects against theta.

## Caveats

1. **n=13.** Every pattern is built on a small sample. Each new V-day is an
   opportunity to update the library. False confidence from small samples is the
   primary risk.

2. **Survivorship bias.** This library only contains confirmed V-days — days where
   the drop DID recover. CONTINUATION days (where the stop-loss fires) are not
   represented. The stop-loss must be relied upon to handle those.

3. **The trough is identified ex-post.** In real-time, Steve doesn't know the trough
   is in. He waits for bounce confirmation, which costs some of the initial move.
   The premium trajectory assumes entry at T+5min, which may understate the real cost.

4. **Premium data is from 0DTE naked calls.** Steve's actual trade structure
   (butterfly, spread, etc.) has a different premium curve. The ES price patterns
   and scare dip findings are trade-structure-agnostic, but the premium numbers
   are specific to naked 0DTE calls.

5. **No bid-ask data.** Actual fill prices may differ from last-traded prices,
   especially during volatile dips. Slippage during scare dips could be significant.
