## 5. §5 — where the blotter collides, and what to change

The design is right and I want it. Six collisions, in the order they cost.

**5.1 The estimated mark is a correlation, not a conversion — and it cannot
resolve a stop.** §5 says *"the ITM single tracks ES at +0.91"*. Measured,
**+0.91 is a Pearson correlation** between the ES net move and the option's
return at the close (`final-hour-premium-vs-es-2026-08-29.md:31`, column header
*"corr: ES net move → option return at close"*). It says how tightly they move
together; it is not premium points per ES point. The actual conversion is §2 of
that same document — bins of ES points → *median % return at the close* — which
COO's own memo names correctly two lines later. Two consequences:

- The curve is **close-only**. It gives one number per day, at 15:00. It cannot
  produce the intraday premium path a `stop_pts: 0.30` or a `target_pct: 25`
  needs. On an estimated day the only exit a row can honestly resolve is `time`.
- A per-minute proxy is a build, not a flag: delta × ES move with the decay term,
  validated against the 276 printed days. That is a bead, and it is the
  precondition for estimated rows carrying anything but a time exit.

**5.2 Pooling estimated and printed rows makes P&L a function of the OPRA
pull.** This is the one that changes what stage 3 can claim. Measured
(`final-hour-premium-vs-es-2026-08-29.md:90`): on right-direction days the 0.30
cut fires **before the first +25% print on 82% of them** for the ~10 ITM single —
median heat −14%, worst −86% — and that is measured *from prints*, print to
print, with sub-100-second gaps. On a day marked from ES at minute resolution
the same 0.30 stop fires close to never. So a blotter that pools the two lanes
reports a rule as profitable on the 15 estimated August days and stopped-out on
the printed days, for no reason but which days got pulled. The `estimated: true`
flag is necessary and not sufficient: **rows must be split by mark path in every
aggregate, never only marked in a different face on the page.** The 82% figure is
also COO's own stated reason for scoring as trades at all, so this is the
finding's own machinery turning on it.

**5.3 The row schema drops the excursion.** `pnl_pts`, `pnl_usd`, `exit_reason` —
no MFE, no MAE. `final_hour_premium.py` already computes both per leg, and
discarding them is the exact defect st-fpc4 was written to fix: *"the win rate
discards the excursion entirely… add distance-run as a first-class outcome
alongside the touch verdict."* Add `mfe_pts` and `mae_pts` to the row. They are
free — the leg dictionary already holds them.

**5.4 The grid and the pre-registration cannot both be live at once.** A rule
declares `exit: {stop_pts, target_pct, time}` and `registered: <sha>`, and the
harness sweeps a stop × target grid beside it. If the grid output is visible when
`exit:` is chosen, the pre-registration is decoration — st-g0jo's discipline is
*"written before the first run; do not tune."* Constraint, and it is cheap: the
`registered` sha must predate the first grid run **on that rule**, a test asserts
`git log` order, and the grid is a separate surface that can never rewrite `exit:`
without a new `registered` sha and a new row set. A rule whose declared exit was
changed after a grid run is reported as re-registered, not as the same rule.

**5.5 The blotter's grid is not st-fpc4's grid.** st-fpc4 grades **recognizer
confirmations** — 2,312 rows, both sides — in **ES points** against the canon's
**structural** stop and target (the flush extreme, computed and discarded at
emission today). The blotter's grid is **premium** exits on registered rules over
≤40 rows. Same word, different population, different unit, different question.
Building one does not satisfy the other, and st-fpc4 must not be closed against
the blotter. Say so in st-uc23's description so a later session cannot conflate
them.

**5.6 The blotter is a third surface, not Stage 4 reshaped.** st-g0jo Stage 4 is
two things (`docs/plans/2026-08-28-final-hour-acuity.md:96-103`): (a) *"a page
generated every session at 14:00, 14:30 and 14:45 CT, carrying each lens' call
and its measured hit rate beside it"* — live, pre-trade, before the hour runs;
and (b) a drill in which **Steve** makes the delta call from the replayed 14:00
state and is scored — *"That is the acuity — his call, scored against three
hundred final hours, on his time."* The blotter scores **rules**, after the fact.
It replaces neither, and (b) is the reason st-g0jo exists at all. Accepted: the
blotter lands beside the master-reference work rather than after it, and its first
rows are the two survivors. Amended: it is Stage 5, the 14:00 page and the drill
stay as Stage 4, and the blotter's `replay` field links into the drill page
rather than standing in for it.

**5.7 Two small ones.** The example row id `2026-08-25-orb-target-1-1` cannot
exist: `orb-target-1` is a `management-rule` (take Target 1; skip if a GEX wall
sits between price and it) with no entry condition, the plan's own header spec
puts `rule:` on *"type regime-rule/setup"*, and ORB is an 08:30–10:00 CT play that
the final-hour lane never sees. Use `2026-08-25-footprint-up-1445-1`. And rows
under `data/blotter/` sit apart from every other measurement output under
`data/measurement/` — `data/measurement/blotter/` costs nothing and keeps one
tree.
