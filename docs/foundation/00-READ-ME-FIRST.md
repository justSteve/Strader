## Why this series exists

Learning to read a volume footprint chart looked like a self-contained exercise. It
isn't. A footprint chart is a **capstone** — it compresses half a dozen underlying
ideas into one dense picture, and every element on it (the colors, the numbers, the
bar widths, the levels drawn across it) is shorthand for a concept that has to exist
in your head *first*. Without the concepts, the chart is decoration. With them, it's
a sentence you can read.

This series builds those concepts in order, from the ground up. Nothing in a later
document depends on anything that wasn't explicitly built in an earlier one.

**House rules for the whole series:**

- Every term is defined the first time it appears, in plain language.
- No abbreviations or code names (like the drill deck's scenario codes) until the
  full idea has been established — the code is always introduced as a *label for a
  thing you already understand*, never as the thing itself.
- Everything stays pointed at one goal: **developing a read on /ES — the E-mini S&P
  500 futures contract — as the proxy for trading SPX options.** No detours into
  stocks, crypto, or general trading theory.

## How to know you're ready to move on

Each document ends with a short **Check yourself** list — questions only; there
are no answers anywhere in this series. When you finish a document, type your
answers into whatever Strader session is open, in your own words. Strader judges
them against the document, probes the gaps, and gives a plain ready-or-re-read
verdict before you move on. If the answers won't come in your own words, the next
document will feel like compressed claims you weren't party to — go back instead;
the series is short enough to re-read.

## Where this sits relative to the drills

The drill deck (`docs/drills/scenario-catalog.md`) and the skill ladder
(`docs/drills/skill-ladder.md`) assume everything in this series. The right order of
operations is:

1. **This series** — the field of play (reading, no screen time required)
2. **Drill units 0–1** — pace and single-bar reading on the replay screen
3. **Drill units 2+** — the level-engagement scenarios

The drills turn knowledge into reflex. This series is what makes the drills
legible in the first place.
