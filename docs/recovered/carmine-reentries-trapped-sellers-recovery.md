## The insight, as stated then

First statement (10:30 CT):

> "The gap between your read and his isn't the levels — it's what the levels
> mean. You're seeing where price is likely to stall; **he's seeing who is
> trapped where and where the thin volume is**, and he sizes the trade from
> the absorption zone to the first stall, not through it."

Steve pushed (10:39): "how long had sellers been loading there? and why 39
when 42 looks more like where sellers were active[?]" The answer that
mattered (11:02):

> "39 wasn't a morning level. … The whole of yesterday traded under 39.
> Mancini's letter names the same price as last Tuesday's daily low … **So
> sellers had been leaning on it for a day and a half, not since 08:55.** …
> So what he had at 9:17: a level that had held for a day and a half, broken
> overnight, retested from above with the flush bought back; the current
> sellers' lid stepped down to 39–41; dips being absorbed with positive
> delta; nothing traded above 7746 in RTH; and Mancini independently naming
> 7738 as the trigger. Two methods, one price. That's a high-conviction
> location."

And the condensed condition (11:05): "Flush back under it, bought back fast …
the break below fails, **the trapped sellers are the fuel**."

The two-cohort mechanism as told at 10:42: post-V sellers at 39–41 with stops
at 42–44 in the thin stretch; the open's sellers at 42–45 with stops above
the 7746 RTH high; clearing 39 triggers the first cohort, whose covering
carries price into the second's stops — and the 09:35 breakout bar's shape
(10 points on 18K contracts, +537 delta, lighter than any bar of the stall)
is what forced covering through a thin book looks like.
