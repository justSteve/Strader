## Evaluator protocol

You are evaluating **possession of the model, not recall of the text**.
Grade each answer: **owned** (mechanism correct, own words) / **borrowed**
(right words, shaky mechanism — probe once with a follow-up) / **missing**.
Rules:

1. Accept any phrasing that preserves the mechanism; reject fluent phrasing
   that inverts it. The compound-term convention is graded gently (bare
   "flush" in speech is fine when context disambiguates) — but a *wrong
   compound* (calling a stall-stage a micro-stall) is a mechanism error.
2. Never stack hints. One follow-up probe per question, then grade.
3. Direction Inversion Watch applies: any answer whose direction anchor is
   flipped is graded missing even if internally coherent — and flagged
   plainly.
4. Mastery bar: 10 of 12 owned, and Q4/Q7/Q10 (the discrimination trio)
   must all be owned — those three are the seat's actual job.
5. Record results per question in the session log; close by naming the two
   weakest answers regardless of pass/fail.

## The questions

1. An atom scores effort 95th percentile, effect 10th percentile. Which cell,
   and what is the market doing in plain words?
2. Same atom: displacement near zero but travel of five points. What does the
   travel-ratio add to the story that displacement alone hides — and why did
   this force us to give effect two faces?
3. Why is force not one of the matrix axes, and which archetype exists only
   because force and direction can disagree?
4. **(discrimination)** At a contested level you see thrust, parry, and you
   cannot yet tell more. Name the two stories you might be in, what
   distinguishes them, and *when* you are allowed to know. What should you
   do in the meantime?
5. "One in five classifications is a literal coin flip." Is that a defect of
   the taxonomy or a finding about the market? What does the vocabulary
   require you to *say* when a label is in the coin-flip grade-band?
6. Cutpoint versus threshold — which sorts, which acts? Give one real example
   of each from the system, and say why bare "cut" is banned from
   definitions.
7. **(discrimination)** 7/22's afternoon leg: atoms nearly all F4-dead, leg
   grades F1. Resolve the paradox, and state the speaking rule it teaches.
8. Describe the zigzag decomposition's drawing rule in your own words,
   including what happens to swings smaller than the threshold — and why the
   4-point opening trap at 7533 is not its own leg.
9. "Legs die hot." What measurement says so, what is the number, and what is
   the honest exception rate? Why does this change what you expect a turn to
   *look* like?
10. **(discrimination)** The V-signature wins 77.5% versus a 46.9% base — so
    why is it not a live entry rule today? Name the two structural reasons
    its inputs are hindsight, and what "turning it live" would require.
11. Your existing words — sweep, absorption, divergence — did they survive
    the taxonomy? Where do they live now, and what tier will your yea/nay
    decisions actually be asked about?
12. State your seat in one sentence each: what you are the authority on,
    what hindsight is the authority on, and what the machine watches.
